The borders, which you were right about

Two separate things were putting the lines in the wrong place, and I caused both of them.

1. I was throwing away detail to save space

The build step simplified the border shapes and left a note saying the error was too small to see. That was true only until you zoom in, which is the entire complaint. I finally measured what the simplification was buying:

SettingWorst errorFile size
Simplified (what shipped)223 metres6.8 MB
Full detail (now)1.1 metres8.3 MB

It was charging 223 metres of accuracy to save 1.5 MB, on a map whose entire job is showing you where things are. That was a bad trade and it is gone.

2. The lines were drawn at sea level while the ground was not

Yesterday I flattened the border lines to make the globe faster, and wrote in the code that it made no visual difference. It makes no difference looking straight down. The moment you tilt the camera, a line drawn at sea level under a mountain lands in the wrong place on screen.

Measured at Four Corners, where four states meet on 1,477 metres of plateau:

Camera angleHow far off the line was
Straight down0 pixels
Tilted 30 degrees9 pixels
Tilted 45 degrees13 pixels
Tilted 60 degrees16 pixels

Perfect from directly overhead, which is exactly why every automated check passed it. The lines now lie on the terrain when you are close enough to see terrain, and go flat when you pull back to orbit where flat is both correct and nine times faster.

What does not work

The Android back button still closes the whole app.

It should close whatever dialog is open first. This is the first thing a store reviewer presses. I found one reason it was failing, fixed that, and it still fails, so it is not root caused yet.

Startup freezes for about 8 seconds.

Measured at phone size: the app stops responding for 8 seconds while it builds 395,238 border points, with one single freeze of over 4 seconds. That is almost certainly why the back button is not being registered in time, so it is one problem, not two. Putting the simplification back would undo your border fix, so the answer is to build the borders a piece at a time instead of all at once.

All five test suites pass with both of those defects present.

109 checks, all green. They run on a desktop browser that has no back button, and none of them watches for the app freezing. A phone-shaped window is not a phone. That gap gets closed next.

The part that cost the most time

The app looked broken on Android for about three hours and was not.

Android's own screenshot tool showed a completely black globe. The browser developer tools showed a white oval of horizontal stripes. Two different tools, two different wrong answers, about the same picture. Neither one can read back the kind of graphics surface this app draws into.

Reading the pixels straight out of the graphics card showed the Earth rendering correctly the entire time. I had spent those hours turning off lighting, atmosphere, imagery and every data layer, chasing a fault that only existed in the camera I was using to look at it.

Worse, my first round of tests changed each setting without undoing the last one, so even the one result that looked meaningful was not attributable to anything. That is written down now so the next session does not repeat it.
