The licensing problem, which I would look at hardest

The airspace and airport data is OpenAIP, licensed CC-BY-NC-SA. Non-commercial, share-alike. Your own .gitignore already says "redistribution restricted" next to it.

A store listing is distribution, and the non-commercial clause applies whether or not the app is free. This has to be resolved before submission, not after a rejection.

The fix is free and public domain: the FAA publishes airports, navaids and airspace on a 28-day cycle with no restrictions. It is real work because the format is fixed-width government data rather than GeoJSON, but it removes the problem permanently. I can do this without needing anything from you.

One thing you should know about the Apple side

Submitting to the App Store requires a Mac to build and sign. You do not have one. That is not a small footnote, it is a purchase or a rented build service before anything reaches iOS. Google Play has no such requirement, so Android can ship first and probably should.

What got fixed today

Four commits, and the first explains the rest.

Your browser was running two-day-old code

The service worker served cached JavaScript first and fetched the update for next time, so every reload ran the previous build. Three rounds of verified fixes never reached you. The server was already sending no-cache headers, which made it look handled, but a service worker sits above the HTTP cache and ignores them entirely.

The white disc was a hole through the planet

Not a missing map layer. The free elevation service stops at 85 degrees, and terrain defines the globe's whole surface structure, so above that latitude there was no ground at all and you were seeing the sky on the far side. The poles now use a smooth sphere past 84 degrees.

Terrain was costing 10x the frame rate

Measured with the camera moving: 6137 ms per frame with terrain and borders on, 964 ms with neither. Terrain now loads on the way down, and 13,098 border lines stopped being draped over terrain for no visible benefit. Default view: 6137 ms to 569 ms.

The radar panel was showing you our engineering problems

Two paragraphs about HTTP 503 responses and commercial lightning feeds, a switch that could never be turned on, and six buttons that did nothing. All gone.

Honest verdict

The app is in good shape as an app. It is not in any shape as a product you can list, and the gap is not the globe: it is hosting, licensing, a native shell, two developer accounts and a Mac.

Android first is the cheaper, faster path and I would take it.
