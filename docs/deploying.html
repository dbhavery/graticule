Deploying Graticule

Two pieces, deployed separately on purpose. Written 14 August 2026.

1. The public pages. Already live.

Done. The privacy policy and support page are on Vercel at https://graticule.vercel.app, free, and verified public with no login wall. Those are the two URLs the Play listing names.

They are kept off the backend deliberately: Play needs them to resolve for as long as the listing exists, and a page that depends on a running server is a compliance problem the first time the server is down.

py -V:3.13 scripts/build_site.py
cd site-dist
vercel deploy --prod --yes

build_site.py copies web/privacy.html and web/support.html rather than keeping a second copy, so the pages that ship are the pages legal_pages_test.py checks. Re-run both commands after any edit to either page.

To take it down: vercel remove graticule --yes.

2. The backend. Needs your account.

This part is yours, because it needs a Fly.io account and a payment method, and neither of those is mine to create. Everything else is written and committed: Dockerfile and fly.toml are in the repository root.

The container has never been built. Docker is not installed on this machine, so the Dockerfile is careful but unproven. Fly builds remotely, so the first fly deploy is where it gets exercised. Expect to fix something on the first run; send me the output.

Four commands, after installing flyctl and signing in:

fly auth login
fly apps create graticule
fly deploy
fly logs

Then point the release build at it and rebuild:

py -V:3.13 scripts/android_build.py --api-base https://graticule.fly.dev --release --aab

Why this shape

The backend cannot be serverless, and that is structural rather than a preference. Its feed loops are long-lived asyncio tasks polling about twenty upstreams on their own schedules, and the state they build lives in memory. A platform that scales to zero stops the feeds. A platform that scales out multiplies the polling against donated ADS-B aggregators, which is rude and eventually gets the app blocked. So: one machine, always up, never more than one. fly.toml sets auto_stop_machines = false and min_machines_running = 1 for exactly that reason, and the comments there say so, so nobody turns autoscaling on later thinking it is free performance.

1 GB because the process was measured at 356 MB resident with every feed loaded. That is a measurement, not a guess, and 512 MB would have been tight.

Fly over a cheaper VM because a Hetzner box saves about $2 a month and costs operating system patching, TLS renewal and firewall upkeep. That is a bad trade before the app has users.

If you would rather not pay anything yet

The alternative is a Cloudflare Tunnel from your own machine, which is free and gives a public HTTPS hostname with no inbound ports. It is a real option and I did not choose it, because a store-listed app whose backend reboots when you reboot is a review problem, and because the box is already shared with GPU work. Say the word and I will set it up instead; the app code does not change either way.
